The [77th International Astronautical Congress (IAC 2026)](https://www.iac2026.org/iac-2026), held in **Antalya, Türkiye, from 5 to 9 October 2026**, brought together the international space community to share research, discuss future exploration and explore opportunities for cooperation.

**The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (PolyU)** participated through project exhibitions and academic forums. Its delegation showcased **10 ongoing space-related research projects**, including TASLAB's quadruped robot project for lunar exploration. TASLAB PhD student **Xiangru Wang** joined the delegation to introduce the project and PolyU's wider space research to congress participants.

### Perspectives from the congress

The congress offered an opportunity to learn about the wider direction of space exploration through presentations and exhibitions from across the international space community. **Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut**, outlined plans for the next phase of China's crewed space programme, including the expansion of the China Space Station and preparations for a crewed lunar landing by 2030, as [reported from the congress](https://aviationweek.com/space/space-exploration/china-details-space-station-evolution-lunar-mission-progress). Members of the PolyU delegation also had a group photograph with him during the congress.

### PolyU's aerospace research and academic exchanges

The delegation's participation came as PolyU expanded its aerospace research through the newly established **PolyU Aerospace Research Academy (PARA)**. The Academy supports interdisciplinary research and talent development, technology transfer, and collaboration with industry, government and international partners. [RCDSE is one of PARA's constituent research units](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/research/labs-institutes-centres/), connecting the lunar robotics project with this wider research community. More about the Academy's development is available in [PolyU's announcement](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/rila/news-and-events/news/2026/0630_para/).

On **7 October**, PolyU hosted the Global Networking Forum (GNF) **“Space Governance: Hong Kong's Role and Perspective”**. **Prof. Hongyu Yu**, Executive Director of PARA, delivered the opening remarks, followed by a keynote by **Mr Steve Gao**, Associate Executive Director of PARA. A panel moderated by **Mr Wu Lei** of CGTN brought together representatives from international organisations, universities and industry to discuss space governance, international cooperation and sustainable space activities. The forum highlighted how Hong Kong's research strengths and international connections can contribute to the space sector. [PolyU's congress report](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/rio/news/2026/20261014---polyu-showcases-space-research-excellence-and-hosts-space-governance-forum-at-iac-2026/) provides further details.

PolyU also contributed to the GNF session **[“Space Manufacturing: Driving the Next Revolution in Biotechnology, Advanced Materials, and Human Exploration”](https://www.iafastro.org/events/iac/international-astronautical-congress-2026/iaf-global-networking-forum/wednesday-7-october/space-manufacturing-technology.html)**, where **Prof. Xiewen Wen** of the Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering shared perspectives on space manufacturing. These exchanges complemented the project exhibition by connecting research developments with wider discussions on governance, emerging technologies and international collaboration.

### Sharing PolyU's space research

Through project posters, video presentations and discussions at the PolyU booth, the delegation shared the University's ongoing work with visitors and explored interests in research collaboration. Xiangru helped introduce the lunar robotics project and the wider showcase of 10 projects, highlighting PolyU's research capabilities and engagement with the international space community. The broader PolyU showcase covered areas including satellite technologies, planetary mapping, space robotics, lunar regolith studies and resource utilisation.

### Quadruped robots for lunar exploration

Led by **Prof. Weisong Wen** and supported by seed funding from PolyU's [Research Centre for Deep Space Explorations (RCDSE)](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/deepspaceex/about-deepspaceex/about-deepspaceex/), the project, **“Quadruped Robot Robust Vision-Task-Action Model for Lunar Surface Scientific Exploration,”** investigates how quadruped robots can support future scientific missions on challenging lunar terrain.

The ongoing research focuses on fault-tolerant locomotion, adaptation to lunar gravity and uneven surfaces, and autonomous task execution guided by visual observations and natural-language instructions. It aims to connect high-level scientific exploration tasks with robust robot navigation and control, including operation under joint or actuator failures. Planned validation includes testing with a gravity offload system.

These demonstrations form part of the team's ongoing development towards more resilient and autonomous exploration systems.
