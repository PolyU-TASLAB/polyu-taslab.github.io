We are pleased to share that three papers led by **Jiaqi Zhao**, **Naigui Xiao** and **Bing Han** from the Trustworthy AI and Autonomous Systems Laboratory (TASLAB) have been accepted for presentation at **IEEE/ION PLANS 2027**, to be held on **12-15 April 2027 in Portland, Oregon, USA**. Congratulations to the students and all co-authors!

The papers explore learning-based measurement modelling and integrity monitoring for multi-sensor navigation, covering UWB-inertial positioning and tightly coupled GNSS/visual/inertial navigation.

### CFFG-Transformer: Causal Attention for Learning Measurement Bias and Noise Models in UWB–Inertial Localization

**Authors:** Jiaqi Zhao, Mokui Yang, Xiangru Wang, Feng Huang, Heng Li and Weisong Wen — The Hong Kong Polytechnic University.

This work introduces a causal cross-frame factor-graph Transformer that learns measurement bias corrections and noise models for UWB-inertial localization. By separating the supervision of measurement correction from noise-model learning, the approach aims to prevent learned corrections from absorbing state-estimation errors. Evaluations on the public UTIL dataset demonstrate improved positioning accuracy under challenging propagation conditions, including tests with reduced anchor configurations.

**Session:** D3 — AI and Machine Learning for PNT.

[Read the official abstract](https://www.ion.org/plans/abstracts.cfm?paperID=17541)

### Solution-Separation Integrity Monitoring for UWB–Inertial Fixed-Lag Smoothing with Explicit Prior-Fault Hypotheses

**Authors:** Naigui Xiao and Weisong Wen — The Hong Kong Polytechnic University.

This paper addresses a safety challenge in UWB-inertial fixed-lag smoothing: an undetected ranging fault can be absorbed into the marginalization prior and continue to affect later position estimates. The proposed multiple-hypothesis solution-separation monitor explicitly considers in-window anchor faults, prior-only faults and faults spanning the marginalization boundary. It formulates fault detection, exclusion and protection-level computation with the prior treated as a potentially faulty source of information.

**Session:** A5 — Inertial Navigation and Multisensor Integration.

[Read the official abstract](https://www.ion.org/plans/abstracts.cfm?paperID=17763)

### Nonlinearity-Aware Efficient Solution Separation for Integrity Monitoring of Tightly Coupled GNSS/Visual/Inertial Navigation in Urban Canyons

**Authors:** Bing Han — The Hong Kong Polytechnic University and Beijing Institute of Technology; Tuan Li — Beijing Institute of Technology; Zhipeng Wang — Beihang University; and Weisong Wen — The Hong Kong Polytechnic University.

This paper proposes an efficient solution-separation approach for integrity monitoring in urban GNSS/visual/inertial navigation. It reuses the all-measurement factorization to compute satellite-exclusion subsets and selectively relinearizes nonlinear factors when approximation errors could affect monitoring decisions. The method examines how visual geometry influences these errors and seeks to balance computational efficiency with reliable fault monitoring.

**Session:** B6b — Integrity and Augmentation 2.

[Read the official abstract](https://www.ion.org/plans/abstracts.cfm?paperID=17653)
